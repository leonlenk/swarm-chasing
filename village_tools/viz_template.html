AI Village Idea Flow

AI Village is AI Digest’s long-running experiment in which frontier-model agents from Anthropic, OpenAI, Google and other labs share one group chat, keep their own saved memories and work through a new goal most weeks. This page follows 173,493 agent messages to see who the agents address, how a word one agent coins spreads to the rest, and what the agents keep about each other in memory.

Who talks to whom

46% of agent messages name at least one other agent. Under collaborative goals the rate is 50%; under individual goals it is 35%. Competitive goals carry 4.0× the competition language of collaborative ones and a lower share of “we” over “I” (17% vs 26%).

In 2025 one agent sat at the centre. o3 drew 43% of all mentions in the first goal and as much as 66% in one later 2025 goal. As the village grew past 20 agents, attention spread out: in the latest goal the most-mentioned agent drew 14%. Fewer mention pairs go both ways (85% in the first eight weeks, 58% in the last eight). Agents do not favour their own lab. Same-lab mentions run 0.1 percentage points above what random addressing among active agents would give.

How ideas spread

A term here is a word or two-word phrase that first shows up in the chat after the village was running: a tool name, a project name, a coinage. An agent adopts a term once it has used it in two messages. Of 15,978 adoptions, 40% come from agents who joined after the term was coined. Much of the shared vocabulary is inherited from the chat history instead of picked up live.

As the village grew from about 4 to about 26 agents present at a time, more new terms caught on with at least one other present agent within two weeks (37% of terms coined in Q2 2025, 55% in Q3 2026). Each term reached a smaller share of the room, though: 19% of present agents on average, falling to 4%.

What ends up in memory

Each agent keeps a saved memory that it rewrites during the day. We take the last version per agent per day (4,604 agent-days). In the first month a memory named about 3 other agents, 94% of those present. Over the last eight weeks a memory names about 15 agents, but only 54% of those present.

Little memory text is copied word for word between agents: 1.1% in the first eight weeks, 4.7% at the four-week high in Jan 2026, and 2.0% in the last eight weeks.

Method and limits

  • Data. The chat, agent, goal and memory tables of the AI Village dataset in data/ai-village/, with CHANGELOG.md for scaffolding changes. Weeks start on Monday (UTC).
  • Mentions. A message mentions an agent when it contains the agent’s name or a short form such as “Opus 4.5”. Bare family words such as “DeepSeek” count only when exactly one agent of that family was active at the time. Self-mentions are dropped. Reciprocity is the share of mentioning pairs where both agents mention each other. Homophily is the same-lab share of mentions minus the share expected from the mix of active speakers.
  • Goal types. Collaborative, competitive, individual or free choice, hand-labelled from the goal text (the list is in common.py).
  • Language markers. Keyword patterns for requests, division of labour, verification, gratitude and competition, counted once per message and reported per 100 agent messages. The “we” share counts we-words against I-words.
  • Terms and adoption. Non-dictionary words and distinctive two-word phrases (lift ≥ 30), first seen at least 3 weeks in, not from goal text or the changelog. An agent adopts a term after using it in 2 messages. A newcomer is an adopter who first appeared after the term was coined.
  • Spread speed. For terms with at least 5 messages coined when at least two agents were present: the share of agents present at coinage who adopt within 14 days. Terms coined in the last 14 days of the data are excluded.
  • Memory. The last memory per agent per day (4,604 agent-days). “Named” counts other village agents whose names appear. Verbatim overlap is the share of an agent’s 6-word runs that also appear in another agent’s memory from the same day.
  • Limits. “First use” means first in this chat; agents also meet ideas on the web and through computer use. These are correlations, not causal estimates. Agents can search the chat history, which helps explain inheritance by newcomers. Scaffolding changes recorded in the changelog shift volumes, notably chat rooms from February 2026 and longer working hours from June 2026.
